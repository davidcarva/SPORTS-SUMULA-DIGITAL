# Global AI cameras and automated video platforms for individual-player highlights (amateur/youth/school, focus on indoor court sports)

Research date: 2026-11-06. Method: web search plus fetching official pages, app store listings and third-party reviews. About 36 tool calls. Several official help-center pages returned 403 or 404 (Veo support, Glory League /about, XbotGo blog). Where a claim rests only on a search-result snippet, it is flagged "(snippet only)". Vendor marketing claims are labelled **[VENDOR CLAIM]**. Independent or third-party facts are labelled **[3RD PARTY]**. Anything older than 2024 is flagged **[PRE-2024]**.

## Q1. Which players produce per-athlete highlights, and how do they identify the player (jersey OCR, tracking, wearable, manual tag, scoresheet)?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of AI-camera products make automatic per-player reels, and most work for outdoor soccer, not indoor courts. Veo Player Spotlight and Trace PlayerFocus are soccer-only. XbotGo's jersey-number lock-on and auto-editing work only in basketball mode. Balltime (Hudl) does AI player identification for volleyball, and users complain about its accuracy. The closest analogue to the Brazilian idea is **Glory League** (Australia/New Zealand): fixed court cameras plus **stats entered live by a courtside scorer** produce a highlight for every point a player scores. It already uses a scoresheet, not AI, to know who scored, but it runs on installed venue cameras rather than a phone.

### Inferences
- Across the market, automatic per-player reels rely on **jersey-number OCR plus a roster/lineup mapping** (Veo, XbotGo), on **opaque AI identification** (Balltime, Trace), or on **human input** (Glory League bench stats, Hudl Assist expert tagging). Only Glory League and Hudl Assist explicitly use human-entered events, and Glory League is the only one that ties a **scorer's point log** to a per-player clip.
- In indoor court sports specifically, per-player auto-highlights are rare: XbotGo (basketball only), Balltime (volleyball only), Glory League (basketball only, venue cameras, AU/NZ only). No product found offers per-player highlights for **futsal or handball**. This is a real gap the Brazilian idea could target.
- Glory League undercuts the claim that the scoresheet-based method is new. The startup's distinct position is narrower: **phone instead of installed cameras**, **multi-sport (futsal/handball/volleyball)**, and **the Brazilian market**.

### Gaps
- No usable information was found on **Once Sport, Vidswap, Mojo, Sportlogiq, SoloShot, Sportscam, OBSBOT, SwishPro**. These were not covered, or searches returned nothing relevant. Sportlogiq and Catapult are pro-focused, so they are probably not direct competitors (not verified).

- Glory League's iPad scoring-app mechanism rests on a search snippet of its About page (404 on fetch) and a 2018 article. Its current workflow and its price per association are unknown.

## Q2. How accurate is automatic player identification in indoor court sports (independent reviews / user complaints)?

### Takeaway
There is very little independent accuracy data. The evidence that exists points to problems: Balltime users say the AI "don't locate the correct player half the time", reviewers call XbotGo player tracking inconsistent, and Veo's own docs admit that covered or duplicate numbers break tracking. Academic jersey-number recognition reaches roughly 87–91% on benchmark datasets, so a meaningful share of clips would be mislabelled without human correction.

### Cited Findings
- Balltime (volleyball) App Store: 3.1/5 from 29 ratings (US). A reviewer on 30 Apr 2025 wrote: "The AI is mediocre at best because don't locate the correct player half the time." Other complaints cover the watermark, switching subscriptions and slow support. **[3RD PARTY – user review]** — [Balltime AI App Store (US)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/balltime-ai/id6450258692)
- Veo's own help center: long hair or anything covering the number "can reduce tracking accuracy", and duplicate numbers on a team "can confuse the system". **[VENDOR ADMISSION]** (snippet only) — [Veo support article](https://support.veo.com/hc/en-us/articles/27698176364817-Overview-of-Veo-Player-Spotlight-How-to-track-and-follow-players-in-your-Veo-recordings)
- XbotGo: reviewers report player-specific tracking as "inconsistent" in real use (snippet only). The SportsSteps review rates basketball tracking as its strongest sport for following the ball, and reports soccer lag on fast counter-attacks. — [SportsSteps Falcon review](https://sportssteps.com/xbotgo-falcon-review-worth-it-filming-kids-games/); [True North VR review](https://truenorthvr.com/xbotgo-chameleon-in-depth-review-tracking-features-and-real-use-results/)
- Academic baseline: jersey-number recognition at 91.4% on a hockey dataset and 87.4% on SoccerNet tracklets. — [arXiv 2405.13896 "A General Framework for Jersey Number Recognition in Sports Video"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13896)
- Research notes that automatic jersey-number detection is still challenging because of camera angles, low resolution, small objects in wide shots, and posture changes. — [SciTePress 2021, Player Identification in Different Sports](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/103417/) **[PRE-2024]**

### Inferences
- Indoor court sports make OCR harder than outdoor soccer in several ways: many players overlap in a small space, they turn their backs to the camera, and a cheap single wide camera has low pixel density per jersey. That likely explains why Veo restricts Spotlight to soccer. This is an inference; no source states it.
- At about 90% per-detection accuracy, a 20-point game produces roughly 2 mislabelled clips. For parents paying for their child's highlights, a wrong-player clip is a visible failure. A scorer's manual entry is likely more accurate when the scorer is competent. The scorer's error rate is not measured in any source.

### Gaps
- No independent, quantified accuracy test was found for Veo, XbotGo, Hudl or Pixellot on basketball, volleyball, futsal or handball.
- No Reddit threads were surfaced on these complaints (searches returned vendor pages).

## Q3. Which solutions are phone-only (no dedicated camera), and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
Phone-based options are a gimbal plus phone (XbotGo Chameleon, Pivo, OWLREC iPhone app) or pure apps (Balltime upload, HomeCourt). XbotGo Chameleon is the only phone-based product found that claims per-player basketball highlights by jersey number. It costs a one-time hardware price with no subscription. Balltime does AI per-player volleyball analysis on any uploaded video for about USD 12–300.

### Cited Findings
- **XbotGo Chameleon**: uses the user's phone. "No Monthly Fee", free app, 20 GB of cloud storage included. Jersey tracking and auto-editing in basketball only. Price was not shown on the page. XbotGo's blog cites a starting price of $349.99 (USD) with no monthly fee (snippet only; the blog page returned 404 when fetched). Outdoor design; 4K only in team modes. — [XbotGo Chameleon page](https://xbotgo.com/pages/xbotgo-chameleon); [XbotGo: Is XbotGo Worth It?](https://xbotgo.com/blogs/how-tos/is-xbotgo-worth-it)
- **XbotGo Falcon** (standalone, not phone): independent reviews report $699–$799 (Standard) and $869.99 (Elite Pack), with a $599 pre-order at CES 2026. Core features are free. The Pro Soccer add-on is paid (price not given). An "AI Analysis" add-on (heatmaps) was announced as paid. — [SportsSteps Falcon review](https://sportssteps.com/xbotgo-falcon-review-worth-it-filming-kids-games/); [SportsSteps Veo vs XbotGo vs Pixellot](https://sportssteps.com/pixellot-vs-veo-vs-xbotgo-best-ai-cameras-youth-sports/)
- **Balltime** (any video, including a phone): App Store in-app purchases (US, 2025–26): Grassroots USD 12.00, Balltime Player USD 20.00 (also seen at USD 25), Premium USD 239.99, Recruiting USD 299.99. — [Balltime App Store (US)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/balltime-ai/id6450258692)

- **OWLREC**: an AI auto-tracking app running on the user's iPhone, sold as a one-time purchase. - No phone-only product found produces per-player highlights for futsal or handball.

### Gaps
- XbotGo Chameleon's current list price was not confirmed on an official product page (only a blog snippet).
- No Brazilian (BRL) prices or local availability were found for any of these products.

## Q5. Funding, acquisitions and market trends 2024–2026

### Takeaway
The market is consolidating around Hudl, a serial acquirer (Balltime, FastModel, Titan Sports, SportContract in 2025; TeamUp in Jul 2026), and Pixellot (USD 35M raised Nov 2025, pivoting to revenue sharing). Low-cost phone and gimbal entrants such as XbotGo are growing at the bottom of the market. Business models are moving from software licences to revenue sharing and parent subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- **Hudl acquired Balltime** (Tel Aviv, AI volleyball video) in Feb 2025. Terms undisclosed. Balltime served more than 12,000 teams at the time (snippet). — [Business Wire, 6 Feb 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250206384625/en/Hudl-Expands-Volleyball-Focus-Through-Game-Changing-Acquisition-of-Balltime); [YSBR](https://youthsportsbusinessreport.com/the-future-of-volleyball-analytics-inside-hudls-acquisition-of-balltime/)
- Hudl's other 2025 acquisitions: FastModel Sports (Mar 2025), Titan Sports (described as its 16th acquisition), SportContract. — [Business Wire, 11 Mar 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250311006384/en); [Mergr – Titan Sports](https://mergr.com/transaction/hudl-acquires-titan-sports); [Hudl blog – SportContract](https://jobs.hudl.com/blog/hudl-acquires-sportcontract)
- Hudl acquired TeamUp (Fund2Save LLC, fundraising software, rebranded Hudl Fundraising) on 7–8 Jul 2026. Total acquisition counts conflict (11 vs. 19). — [Dealroom TeamUp](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/teamup_11); [PitchBook Hudl](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/56408-68)

### Inferences
- Hudl's Balltime purchase shows incumbents buying AI volleyball analysis. A Brazilian entrant in volleyball or futsal could become an acquisition target, or face a Hudl-bundled competitor.
- Pixellot's shift to revenue sharing and falling streaming costs mean hardware-heavy vendors are fighting on price. A phone-based product benefits from this trend but would compete with "good enough" gimbals.
